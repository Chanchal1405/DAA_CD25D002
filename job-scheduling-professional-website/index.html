OVERVIEW

What is Job Scheduling?

Job Scheduling with Deadlines is a Greedy algorithm that schedules jobs to maximize total profit while respecting their deadlines.

Each job has a job name, a deadline and a profit. Every job takes one unit of time. A job earns its profit only when it is completed on or before its deadline.

01

Job

A task that needs exactly one unit of time.

02

Deadline

The latest time slot in which the job can be completed.

03

Profit

The reward earned when the job is completed on time.

HOW IT WORKS

Greedy Scheduling Workflow

Three simple decisions drive the algorithm.

01

Sort by Profit

Arrange jobs from highest profit to lowest profit.

02

Find a Slot

Search backward from the job's deadline for a free slot.

03

Schedule or Skip

Place the job if a slot exists; otherwise skip it.

ANALYSIS

Complexity

T

Time Complexity

O(n log n + n × d)

Jobs are sorted by profit and then each job may search backward through available slots.

S

Space Complexity

O(d)

A time-slot structure is used to keep track of scheduled jobs.

G

Greedy Choice

Highest Profit First

The algorithm considers jobs in decreasing order of profit.

REAL-WORLD CONTEXT

Applications

Scheduling concepts appear in many planning problems.

01

Task Scheduling

Prioritizing tasks that must be completed within available time.

02

Project Planning

Choosing activities under deadlines and limited resources.

03

Production Planning

Scheduling production tasks according to completion deadlines.

04

Resource Allocation

Assigning limited time slots to activities based on priority.

KEY TAKEAWAY

Make the highest-profit feasible choice first.

By sorting jobs by profit and placing each selected job in the latest available slot before its deadline, the standard Job Scheduling problem can be visualized as a clear sequence of Greedy decisions.
